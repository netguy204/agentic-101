# Practice: one week with the sheet

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/fluency_session_01 - Session 1 practice assignment -->

**About 55 minutes, spread across the week. Cap it at 90.**

This isn't homework, and there's nothing to hand in beyond bringing the
sheet to Session 2. It's a week of noticing. The list you started in the
session came out of memory, and memory is a bad witness about your own
work — it remembers the interesting parts and quietly deletes the twenty
minutes you spend every morning moving numbers between two places. Your
actual week is a much better witness.

**Where you're heading:** 8–10 entries by Session 2, each with the check
column filled in. That's enough that Session 6 has real choices to pick
your capstone from.

---

## The time budget

| When | How long | What |
|---|---|---|
| End of each working day (×4) | 5 min | Add anything repetitive you caught yourself doing |
| Once, mid-week | 15 min | The check test (below) |
| Once, before Session 2 | 20 min | The consolidation pass (below) |
| | **55 min** | |

The remaining half hour is deliberate slack. If your setup didn't work,
that's where your remediation slot with your facilitator goes.

---

## 1. The daily pass (5 minutes × 4)

Keep the sheet where you'll see it — pinned by your desk, or a note on
your phone. At the end of each working day, ask one question:

> **What did I do today that I've done before?**

Write it down. First two columns only, roughly: the task and how often,
and one sentence on what "done right" looks like. Don't judge it, don't
decide whether it's automatable, don't fill in the check column yet.

Two things worth catching that people usually miss:

- **The waiting and the chasing.** Following up, nudging, checking whether
  someone did the thing. It's rarely on anyone's list of tasks and it's
  often the biggest number on the sheet.
- **The stuff before the work.** Getting a file into the right shape,
  fixing someone else's formatting, finding last month's version. The
  fifteen minutes before you can start the thing you were going to do.

If a day produces nothing, write "nothing new" and stop. That's a real
answer and it takes ten seconds.

---

## 2. The check test (15 minutes, once)

Pick **two entries you marked `not sure`** in the session. The next time
you do either of those tasks, pay attention to one thing: **what did you
actually look at to know it was right?**

Not what you'd theoretically check. What your eyes actually went to. It's
usually smaller and more specific than people expect — a single total, a
name that should be on the list, a date that's always wrong when it's
wrong, the one row you always eyeball because it caught you out once.

Write that down in the check column, then change the entry to **in** or
**out** honestly.

This is the highest-value fifteen minutes of the week. A `not sure` is an
entry you can't use, and the only way to resolve it is to watch yourself
doing the work. Two resolved entries beat five new vague ones.

---

## 3. The consolidation pass (20 minutes, before Session 2)

Sit down with the whole sheet once:

1. **Fill in the check column for everything added during the week.** Same
   bar as the session: concrete, or it's a `not sure`.
2. **Split anything too big.** If a row can't be pictured as a single
   activity, it's two or three rows. "Month-end reporting" becomes "pull
   the regional numbers", "rebuild the deck", "write the commentary" —
   and those three probably don't all have the same answer in the check
   column, which is exactly why splitting matters.
3. **Add the time.** Roughly how long, roughly how often, for every row.
   Session 6 picks capstones partly on this, and "twice a year, 20
   minutes" is a bad capstone no matter how automatable it is.
4. **Star your three most annoying entries.** Not the most automatable —
   the most annoying. Annoyance is a surprisingly good predictor of what
   you'll still be using a month after the course ends.

---

## Optional, if you want more

Pick **one** entry marked **in** and write its check column out properly:
half a page briefing a new joiner on how to tell whether this work was
done right. Not how to do it — how to *judge* it. What good looks like,
what the common mistakes are, what you'd never sign off.

That page is worth writing for its own sake, and there's a second reason
you'll appreciate in Session 5: the same page, more or less unchanged, is
how you tell an agent what you expect. Judgment written down once,
applied every time. You don't need to know anything about the tooling
today — just write it as you'd write it for a person.

---

## Bring to Session 2

- **Your sheet**, with 8–10 entries and the check column filled in.
- **A working setup.** If yours failed, your remediation slot is before
  Session 2 — take it. Session 2 is the session that needs it, and it's
  the only one where arriving broken actually costs you something.

## If the week gets away from you

Do the consolidation pass and nothing else. Twenty minutes with the three
entries you already have beats arriving with nothing because you didn't
have time to do it properly. The sheet is a working document for the whole
course — it doesn't have to be finished, it has to exist.
