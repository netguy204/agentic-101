# Practice: two real ones

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/fluency_session_02 - Session 2 practice assignment -->

**About 60 minutes, spread across a week. Stop at 90.**

Two of the three parts use work you were going to do anyway, so pick tasks
that annoy you.

By the end you'll have delegated three times: once in the lab and twice on
your own. Session 3 starts from the one that went worst, so a bad run is
useful here.

| When | How long | What |
|---|---|---|
| Within 48 hours of the lab | 20 min | The solo lap |
| Twice during the week | 15 min each | Two real delegations |
| Once, before Session 3 | 10 min | A worksheet on the one that went worst, then submit |

---

## 1. The solo lap (20 minutes, within 48 hours)

**You'll need:** your agent page.

Run the whole loop again, on your own:

> **delegate → check → keep it, or undo it**

1. Ask for something small: a packing list, a tidy-up of the file from the
   lab, or a summary of a file under **Your files**.
2. Read the steps the agent took, then open the file.
3. Keep it if it's right. If it isn't, choose **Undo the last request**, or
   go back with **Earlier versions**.

**Done when:** you've run the loop once, start to finish, with nobody
helping.

If you're stuck for more than five minutes, stop and get in touch with your
instructor: choose "Stuck on this session?", then "Send to your
instructor".

**If you run pi on your own computer:** keep the
[terminal card](handouts/terminal-card.md) open; its
[When you feel lost](handouts/terminal-card.md#when-you-feel-lost) section
covers most of the ways this can go sideways. In `~/course-practice`: type
`commit` (name it something like "before Tuesday"), then `pi`, and ask for
something small. Read the tool boxes, open the file, type `/quit`, then
`commit` to keep it or `revert` to undo it.

## 2. Two real ones (15 minutes each)

**You'll need:** your
[toil inventory sheet](../session_01_mental_models/handouts/toil-inventory.md#the-sheet)
and your agent page.

What you type, and the contents of any file the agent reads, is sent to the
model provider. **Undo the last request** and **Earlier versions** (or
`commit` and `revert`) recover files in the agent's folder only.

A real task must pass **all four questions** before you delegate it:

1. **Evaluable:** Can you tell a reasonable result from an unreasonable one
   and name the check you will apply? If not, do not delegate it.
2. **Authorized:** Are you allowed to delegate this task and send its data to
   the course model provider? If someone else owns the data or process, ask
   first.
3. **Appropriate and non-consequential:** Is this suitable draft or
   preparation work, rather than a consequential decision about a person,
   money, access, employment, health, legal rights, or another high-stakes
   outcome? The agent may prepare evidence; a responsible person decides.
4. **Human-approved before external action:** Can a person review the actual
   output and explicitly approve it before anything is sent, published,
   purchased, scheduled, or otherwise acted on outside the practice folder?
   If not, keep the task synthetic.

Here's an example. A supplier emails asking for three things. The agent can
read the thread and the figures and draft the reply. You check the figures,
fix what's wrong, and decide whether it goes. If it goes, you send it. The
agent drafts, and you decide and send.

Pick **two rows on your sheet marked *in*** that pass all four questions,
other than the one you used in the lab. The next time each task comes
round:

1. Ask your agent for what you want in a plain sentence. Your row's *What
   "done right" looks like* column is the instruction. If the task needs a
   file, add it with **Upload files** first.
2. **Apply the check in your row's *How I'd check* column to the actual
   output.**
3. Keep the result as a draft until the right person has reviewed it and
   approved any action outside the agent's folder.
4. Keep the files, or undo them with **Undo the last request** or **Earlier
   versions**.

**If you run pi on your own computer:** in `~/course-practice`, `commit`
first, then `pi`. Afterwards, `/quit`, then `commit` to keep the files or
`revert` to undo them. The [terminal card](handouts/terminal-card.md) has
every command.

**Your data.** Use one of these:

| Option | When to use it |
|---|---|
| **Describe the shape and let it invent the data**, as in the lab | Anything sensitive, or hard to get out of the system it lives in |
| **Add a small, real, non-sensitive extract** with **Upload files** (or copy it into `~/course-practice`) | Only if you're allowed to send it to the model provider |
| **A made-up version of the real task** | When you want the practice more than the output |

If you're unsure whether something is safe to send, ask whoever owns it
first. Nothing here requires you to send anything you have doubts about.

If the agent struggles, or needs something it can't see, write down what
was missing. Session 3 uses it.

**Done when:** you've delegated two real tasks and applied your *How I'd
check* column to each result. Tick both rows on your sheet.

## 3. One worksheet, on the worst one (10 minutes, before Session 3)

**You'll need:** a fresh
[what-did-it-do worksheet](handouts/what-did-it-do.md).

Take the delegation that went least well and fill in your worksheet: all
three boxes and
[the one question to carry away](handouts/what-did-it-do.md#the-one-question-to-carry-away)
at the bottom. Under it, write one sentence on why this one went worst.
Session 3 opens with this worksheet.

If all three went well, use the one where the agent made a decision you
hadn't asked it to make.

**Done when:** all three boxes, the question at the bottom and your sentence
are filled in. Then submit your work. The Submit step on the session page
lists exactly what's needed.

---

## Optional: one row marked *out*

Pick a row on your sheet you marked *out*. Ask the agent to do it, then
spend two minutes trying to tell whether the output is right. Write one
sentence on what you'd need to know to check it. Don't use the result for
anything.

---

## If the week gets away from you

Do the solo lap within 48 hours, and submit with the worksheet from the
lab. The loop is what Session 3 builds on.
