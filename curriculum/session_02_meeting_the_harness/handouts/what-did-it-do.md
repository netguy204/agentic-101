# What did it actually do?

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/fluency_session_02 - Session 2 delegation worksheet -->

Fill one of these in after each delegation. Three boxes, in this order,
because the order is the skill: **what it did**, then **what changed**, then
**was that reasonable**.

The agent will also have written you a paragraph explaining itself. Do not
start there. That paragraph describes what it *intended*; the boxes on your
screen and the actual file are what *happened*, and those are the two things
you are learning to read.

## Box 3 — Was it reasonable, and how do I know?

This is the box the whole course is about. Column 4 of your toil inventory —
"how I'd check" — is the method. You wrote it before you had ever seen this
software. Use it.

**The check I applied** (the specific thing I looked at):

>
>

**What it told me:**

- [ ] Fine — it did what I'd have done
- [ ] Fine, but it made a judgment call I should know about: ……………………………
- [ ] Wrong, and my check caught it: ……………………………
- [ ] I couldn't tell

**If you ticked the last box, that is the most valuable line on this page.**
It means the task is outside your judgment boundary as currently framed, and
that is a finding, not a failure. Write down what you would need to know in

## The one question to carry away

**What would I have failed to catch?**

>
>

Not what it got wrong — what it *could* have got wrong in a way your check
wouldn't have found. Session 1's demo turned on exactly this: every
mechanical check passed and the answer was still wrong, and only somebody who
knew the company caught it.

You don't have to answer it today. You do have to keep asking it.
