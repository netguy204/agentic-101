# Before Session 3

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/fluency_session_03 - Session 3 prework -->

**About 25 minutes.** Something to read, one thing to finish, one thing to
choose, and one command to run.

Session 3 is about the delegation that went badly — why it went badly, and
how to write down what an agent needs so it doesn't. It works much better
if you arrive with your own example, so most of this page is making sure
you have one.

---

## 1. Read this (8 minutes)

### Why the bad one went badly

By now you've handed an agent work three times — once in the room, twice on
your own — and at least one of them didn't go the way you wanted. Good.
That's the one this session is built on.

Here is the thing about bad delegations: nearly every one is one of three
things.

- **It didn't know something.** It needed a file, a fact, a date, a rule —
  and it wasn't there. So it guessed. Agents guess politely and confidently,
  and they don't always say so.
- **It decided something.** It made a choice you hadn't asked it to make —
  merged two labels that mean different things where you work, picked a
  date format, filled in a blank — and only sometimes mentioned it. In
  Session 2, most of the room watched it fold *Complete* into *Done*.
- **I couldn't tell.** It might have been fine. You had no way of knowing,
  because there was nothing you could actually look at and check.

That's it. Three reasons. In the session you'll hear the whole room's worst
ones, one sentence each, and watch them sort into those three — and then
we'll name the four things a delegation needs so that none of the three
happens.

### The four things

Think of briefing a capable colleague who cannot see your screen, doesn't
know your company, and will not ask. What do you owe them?

1. **What they must be able to see** — the files, and the facts that aren't
   written down anywhere.
2. **What done right looks like** — you already wrote this, in column 2 of
   your inventory.
3. **What done produces, and where** — a file, a place, a shape.
4. **Where you'll look to check, and what for** — column 4 of your
   inventory, as a step you'll actually take.

In the session you'll write those four down on one page — a **delegation
brief** — for one of your own tasks, run it, find out which of the four let
you down, fix that one, and run it again. Two of the four you've already
got. The other two are the ones that were missing when it went wrong.

### What the hour actually looks like

1. Everyone says one sentence about their worst delegation. We sort them.
2. You write a brief for one of your own tasks.
3. You run it, read what happened, change one thing on the brief, and run it
   again.
4. You write down what your checks would *still* miss — and decide whether
   this is a task you should be delegating at all.

Step 3 is the session. Step 4 is the reason for it.

## 2. Finish the worst one (10 minutes)

Session 2's practice sheet asked you to fill in a
[`what-did-it-do.md`](../session_02_meeting_the_harness/handouts/what-did-it-do.md)
worksheet on whichever of your three delegations went worst, including the
question at the bottom — *what would I have failed to catch?* If you did,
get it out. If you didn't, do it now: ten minutes, all three boxes, honest.

Then write **one sentence** underneath: what you asked for, and what went
wrong. You'll say it out loud in the first ten minutes of the session, so
make it a sentence you'd be comfortable saying. Don't explain or defend it —
just what you asked, and what happened.

> If all three of yours went well, use the one where the agent made a
> decision you hadn't asked it to make. There will be one. If you can't find
> one, say so in your sentence — "mine all went fine and I'm suspicious" is
> a perfectly good sentence, and you'll get your bad one in the room.

## 3. Pick the task you'll brief (5 minutes)

Get your **toil inventory** out. You'll write a brief for one entry during
the session. A good one:

| | Why |
|---|---|
| **Marked `in`** — you could tell good work from bad | Everything on this course stays inside your judgment boundary |
| **Passes the four questions** from Session 2's practice sheet — you can check it, you're allowed to, it isn't a consequential decision, nothing leaves the folder without a person approving it | The brief will run for real in the practice week |
| **Has a file in it** — a list, a table, a set of records, something with rows | The agent works on files; that's what the *inputs* box will name |

The entry your worst delegation came from is the natural choice, if it fits.
Put a star next to whichever you pick. You'll describe the *shape* of its
file in the room, not the real contents — same as Session 2.

**If nothing fits**, say so when you arrive. There are ready-made briefs for
Priya's tasks, and they take exactly the same time.

## 4. Run one command (2 minutes)

Open the course terminal — **Mac:** `Command + Space`, type `Ghostty`,
Enter. **Windows:** Start, type `Terminal`, Enter on **Windows Terminal**
(not "Git Bash"). Then type this and press Enter:

```
course-update
```

It fetches any course fixes published since last time — most weeks it says
**Nothing to do.** If it tells you to close the window and open a new one,
do that. You'll run it again at the start of the session; this is just so
nothing surprises you in the room.

> If it says it couldn't reach the course updates, or anything else goes
> wrong, **stop there and tell your facilitator**. Don't try to fix it. Same
> rule as always: a confused participant is a bug in our instructions, not
> in you, and nothing about this session depends on it.
