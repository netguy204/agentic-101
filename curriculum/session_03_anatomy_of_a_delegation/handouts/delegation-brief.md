# Delegation brief

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/fluency_session_03 - The cross-session delegation brief: inputs, instruction, outputs, checkpoints -->

One page, four boxes. Fill one in before you hand a task to an agent. It
is the written form of what you would tell a capable colleague who cannot
see your screen, does not know your company, and will not ask — so
anything you leave out, they will guess.

Two of the boxes are already on your toil inventory. The **Instruction** is
column 2 — "what done right looks like" — made precise enough to act on.
The **Checkpoints** are column 4 — "how I'd check" — turned into a step you
will actually take. The other two boxes are the ones Session 2's worst
delegation was usually missing.

Print one per delegation. Keep the ones that worked; they are the start of
Session 4.

---

## 1. Inputs — what must it be able to see?

Name the files, and name the facts. If it can't see it, it will guess.
Everything here either sits in the folder already or appears in the
sentence you send — if it is in neither, it is not an input, it is a hope.

| Files it needs (in `~/course-practice`) | Facts it needs (that aren't written anywhere) |
|---|---|
|  |  |
|  |  |

> *Example:* `sample.csv` — the fifteen rows from Session 2. *Facts:* "Done"
> and "Complete" mean different things where I work; today's date is the
> Monday.

---

## 2. Instruction — what does done right look like?

Column 2 of your inventory, made into one sentence it can act on: name the
file *and that it's in this folder*, name the result, say what not to touch.
This is the sentence you will type.

>
>

> *Example:* "Fix the mistakes in `sample.csv` in this folder: one spelling per status, one
> date format, keep every row. Treat 'Done' and 'Complete' as different
> statuses. Tell me what you changed and why."

---

## 3. Outputs — what does done produce, and where?

A file name, a place, a shape. If you can't name it, you can't check it.

>

> *Example:* `sample.csv`, same fifteen rows, in the same folder.

---

## 4. Checkpoints — where will I look, and what for?

Column 4 of your inventory, as a step: *I will open ___ and look at ___.*
Two is better than one. "I'll read it over" is not a checkpoint — say what
you'd be reading *for*.

>
>

> *Example:* Open the file. Count the rows — still fifteen. Read the status
> column — exactly the labels I expect, and "Done" and "Complete" both still
> there.

---

## 5. What would I have failed to catch?

Filled in last, after the brief has run. Name one thing that could go wrong
that your checkpoints would *not* detect. Then either add a checkpoint that
would catch it — or write **out** beside the task, because it sits outside
your judgment boundary for now. Either is a result.

>

---

## Which box let me down, and what I changed

After the first run: which box was wrong — inputs, instruction, outputs or
checkpoints — and the one change you made before running it again. One
sentence. This sentence is the checkpoint for Session 3.

>

---

## How this sheet is used later

| When | What happens to it |
|---|---|
| Session 3 | You write one for a real entry, run it, change one box for a reason you can name, and run it again. |
| Between sessions 3 and 4 | You run it on the real task twice, revising between. Keep it. |
| Session 4 | The brief becomes the first step of a workflow — the same four boxes, repeated, with the checkpoints where they buy the most. |
| Session 6 | Your capstone design is a brief (or several) the cohort reviews. Entries with a strong checkpoints box get picked first. |

Later sessions may add to this sheet. They will not rename or renumber the
four boxes, so anything you write today stays valid to the end of the
course. This sheet is owned by Session 3 and lives in its `handouts/`
folder; later sessions that extend it edit it there.
