# Session 1 — Mental models and the toil inventory

<!-- Chunk: docs/chunks/platform_student_index - The student's index for Session 1, written from its README and PREWORK -->

This is the first session of the course, and it is a conversation rather
than a piece of software. You installed something to get here and you ran
a check that said PASS; today you can forget both. Nothing in this session
asks you to open a terminal or type a command — Session 2 is where the
software starts earning its keep.

What the session is for: it gives you the two distinctions the rest of the
course is built on, and it gets you started on a list of your own work
that every later session draws from.

**By the end you will have** a written list of the repeating work in your
job — at least three entries, each marked with whether you could tell a
good result from a bad one *and* with the actual check written next to it
— and you will be able to say, about one task
on it, why it sits inside or outside what you can judge, naming the check
you would use to find out.

## What it will cost you

| | |
|---|---|
| **The concept track** | About **15 minutes** of listening — four segments, listened to or read, no screen needed; a commute or a walk is the design case. The rest of the concept time is the prework's doing: your five-task list and the setup check, which the track hands back to you. |
| **The lab** | About **92 minutes**, and you are not meant to do it in one go. |

The lab figure includes the practice week. There is no cohort waiting for
you, so there is no "between sessions" — the short practice passes that
follow the main block are simply the last sittings of the lab, and they
are counted in the 92 minutes rather than added on afterwards. You will
get the list of sittings when the lab opens, each with its own honest
number and the one thing you should have at the end of it.

## What you will do

**In the concept track**, four things, in this order: why this moment is
different and why it is different *for you*; what an agent actually is,
and how something that acts on files over several steps differs from
something you chat with; the difference between being able to *do* a piece
of work and being able to *judge* it, which is the distinction the whole
course turns on; and the list you are about to start keeping.

**In the lab**, one sheet — your toil inventory. You empty your repeating
work onto it, then go back over it and write, for each entry, how you
would know if the result were wrong. Then you take a skeptical pass over
what you wrote and change your mind about at least one row. The practice
passes over the following days are short: a few minutes at the end of a
day to add whatever you caught yourself doing again, one pass to settle
the entries you were not sure about, and one longer tidy-up before you
start Session 2.

The sheet is not a warm-up exercise. Later sessions pick tasks off it, and
the final session uses it to decide what you build — including the entries
you correctly ruled out.

## Before you start

The prework is about 25 minutes: a piece of reading, and a list of five
things you did more than once last week. Do the reading first; it makes
the concept track land. The list can be scruffy — you will rewrite it
properly on the sheet.

The prework was written for a room, so it ends by asking you to email a
facilitator two things. On your own, neither needs sending. Your setup
status is the check you already ran to get here. And the second question —
whether you will use your real work or the prepared alternative — is
simply yours to answer: if your work is confidential, or you would rather
not write it down, take the prepared set of tasks instead. It is a
full-quality substitute rather than a consolation prize, and the exercises
take the same time and produce the same thing either way.

## What you hand in

Two things.

1. **Your toil inventory sheet.** At least three entries, each one marked
   *in*, *out* or *not sure* — and for the ones marked *in*, something
   concrete in the check column. Not "I'd read it over": the actual thing
   you would look at.
2. **One task, explained.** Pick a task from your sheet and say whether it
   is in or out of what you can judge, and how you would know if the
   result were wrong. Speak it into a voice note if you would rather talk
   than type; a few sentences of text is equally fine.

Both are required, and it is worth knowing why: three filled rows without
the explanation is a form you filled in, and the explanation without the
rows is a nice conversation. The pair is what the session is for.

## What comes next

Session 2 is the hands-on one: an agent running on your own machine,
changing a real file, while you watch what it actually did. You will bring
one entry from this sheet with you, so it is worth doing the practice
passes before you start it — the sheet is what you point the agent at.
